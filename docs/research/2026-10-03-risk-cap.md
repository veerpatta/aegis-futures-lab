# Practice-account risk cap — 3 October 2026

Paper only. Delayed data. Nothing here touches real money.

## Decision

One trade's risk R goes from **$50 to $100 (1% of the $10,000 practice
account)**. Every other limit keeps its proportion to R:

| Limit | Was | Now | In R |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Full risk per trade | $50 | $100 | 1R |
| Probation risk per trade | $25 | $50 | 0.5R |
| All open trades together | $100 | $200 | 2R |
| Daily loss stop | $200 | $400 | 4R |
| Drawdown lock (and the release gate's stressed-drawdown bar) | $1,000 | $2,000 | 20R |

`lib/paper/policy.ts`, version `paper-risk-2026-10-03`. The owner asked for
the cap to be raised on this analysis's recommendation.

## Why

The research replay sizes every trade against R, and refuses a signal when one
contract alone would risk more. At $50 that refusal was the norm on MNQ, so the
forward evidence only ever contained the tight-stop minority of each method's
signals — a selected sample, not the method. It is also why 8 of the 10
forward intents recorded since 25 September could never close.

every research candidate run unchanged except for the cap, one contract's risk
including $2.40 commission:

$150 adds only a little more coverage, and because sizing is risk-based it
mostly doubles the contract count on small-stop MES trades — more exposure, not
more information. 1% of the account per trade is also the conventional ceiling.

## What it does NOT change

- **No method is better for it.** The same analysis shows every refuted method
  losing more in dollars at a higher cap, because it trades more. Their verdicts
  are unchanged; nothing is tuned.
- The live signal tiers keep their own `EXECUTION.maxRisk` ($160); this policy
  governs only the research replay and the practice account.
- No account state needed migrating: no paper release has ever existed and the
  practice account has never traded.

## Consequences

- `lib/paper/policy.ts` is under the research code hash and inside
  `researchConfig`, so both hashes move. The development measurement re-runs on
  the new configuration and registers new trials (a re-run is a new trial and
  counts as one). `candidate_progress` now reads the latest trial per candidate
  (`db/migrations/20261003_candidate_progress_latest_trial.sql`).
- Separate confirmation must be re-run on the new configuration before any
  candidate could qualify; it is not run automatically (it may need data the
  archive does not yet hold).

## Found while re-measuring: the research hash depended on the operating system

`researchCodeHash()` sorted file paths BEFORE converting Windows backslashes to
forward slashes, so the same code hashed differently on Windows and Linux. The
25 September development measurement was computed on Windows (`affc0974…`);
the live engine runs on Linux and recorded every forward observation under
`921d5fa8…`. `candidate_progress` only counts forward rows whose hash matches the
measurement's, so since 25 September every candidate's forward count was zero
regardless of what traded — not a lack of evidence, a mismatch.

Paths are now normalised before sorting (`scripts/engine/research-code.ts`), and
the hash is identical on both systems (`c933481b…`, pinned in
`tests/research-code-pin.test.ts`, which now guards both platforms). The
development measurement was re-run on Linux after the fix so measurement, engine
and release checks all share one hash.
